Walk-forward validation

The table above scores the one model this site actually serves forecasts from, on a single held-out slice of history. To check whether that single number is representative rather than a lucky draw, this re-trains a fresh, disposable model on an expanding window and re-tests it on the next slice of time it never saw, several times in a row per horizon. Consistent hit rates fold-to-fold mean the model's skill is real and repeatable, not a fluke of which dates happened to land in one holdout.

How to use Lumina Advisors

A quick guide to the site and the terms you'll see on it.

Getting around

  • Jump to a company — use the search box at the top of the page anytime; type a ticker or company name and pick it from the dropdown.
  • Relationship Graph — every company as a node, sized by how confident the model is and colored by sector. Click a node (or search) to open its forecast.
  • All Forecasts — every company in one sortable, filterable table. Click a row to see the full breakdown.
  • Compare — put 2–4 companies side by side: same metrics, same horizon, easy to eyeball.
  • Watchlist — star any company to track it here (sign in to keep it saved between visits). Once you've starred 2+, a diversification check shows sector concentration and any direct supplier/customer/competitor/partner links between your picks.
  • Model Scorecard — the model's actual, honestly-measured accuracy on data it never trained on. Check this before trusting any single call.
  • Data Health — shows, per company and per data field, whether you're looking at live market data or the synthetic demo generator.

Reading a forecast

  • Call — the model's predicted direction (up/down) over the selected horizon.
  • P(up) — the model's estimated probability the price finishes higher at the end of the horizon. 50% means a coin flip; further from 50% means a stronger lean.
  • Expected move — the model's predicted percentage price change over the horizon.
  • Confidence — how strongly the model leans one way, weighted by how accurate this model has actually been historically. Not a guarantee — always check the Scorecard.
  • Drivers — the specific inputs (momentum, sentiment, earnings timing, connected-company spillover, etc.) that pushed the call up or down, and by how much. Hover the ⓘ next to any driver for a plain-English explanation.
  • Live vs. Demo — a green "Live · source" tag means that field is backed by a real market data provider right now; a yellow "Demo" tag means synthetic data is filling in (usually because a provider is temporarily unreachable). See the Data Health tab for the full picture.

What this is (and isn't)

Lumina Advisors models each company's real supplier, customer, competitor, and partner relationships as a graph, and lets momentum, sentiment, and earnings signals flow between connected companies when forecasting. Every call comes with a plain-English rationale so you can see why, not just what. It is not investment advice — it's a statistical model with a tracked, honestly-measured accuracy record. Treat every number as a probability, not a promise.
